# Product brief

## Product promise

**See where reporter capacity is missing, build the right supply, and show whether the work helped.**

Primary user: a manager leading sourcing, screening, and onboarding. Secondary users: internal team members working assigned cases. Reporter network records are not internal employee performance records.

The demo should demonstrate the user's interpretation of the supplied JD: ownership of supply growth, systems, programs, team execution, and reporting. Demand and job delivery are inputs and downstream observations; the app does not claim this role owns forecasting, daily dispatch, or all provider management at Steno.

## Five connected workspaces

| Workspace | Decision | Required working surface |
|---|---|---|
| Markets | What supply is needed, where, and by when? | Demand/coverage summary, requirements breakdown, evidence, dated growth plan |
| Recruiting | Where does relevant supply enter or stall? | Current work, cohort funnel, source comparison, screening and onboarding |
| Reporters | What can the existing network support? | Capabilities, explicit availability, recent work, follow-up |
| Team | Is execution constrained by workload, process, or coaching? | Role-specific goals, owned work, quality samples, coaching follow-through |
| Programs | What should we try, continue, stop, or expand? | Editable program grid, results, decisions, and process drafts |

All markets / LAX / SFO / DFW / ORD / ATL remain the single global market selector. Airport codes are navigation shorthand, never jurisdiction or qualification rules.

## Three different responses to a capacity gap

1. **Confirm existing supply.** A suitable, available reporter exists but has not accepted the request.
2. **Help someone become ready.** A promising candidate has an unresolved verification or onboarding step.
3. **Source or re-engage supply.** The current verified network cannot meet a specific requirement or time.

These are alternatives to investigate, not a fixed algorithm claiming causality. The evidence must distinguish a missing fact from a confirmed mismatch. An unfilled request alone cannot prove that recruiting is the right remedy.

## The target and the outcome are different

For the main scenario, the manager sets a growth goal of **two newly realtime-ready reporters by February 23**. The goal has a frozen definition, a baseline, an owner, and a deadline. It is not calculated by dividing open jobs by names in the database.

Track three separate outcomes beside it:
- reporters who become ready under the required verified criteria;
- specific requests with a valid accepted assignment;
- completed jobs, including first jobs where applicable.

A target edit changes the plan, never the observed records. A completed follow-up does not imply readiness. Readiness does not imply acceptance. Acceptance does not imply completed work.

## What each JD theme demonstrates here

| Supplied JD theme | Product demonstration |
|---|---|
| End-to-end supply and provider growth | Demand-informed market plan linked to people and downstream work |
| New sources, tools, incentives, expert input | Program types and a source comparison; partner deliverables; no fabricated expert endorsement |
| Weekly diagnosis and reporting | Current-week activity and mature cohort results with distinct labels and record drill-downs |
| Team goals, quality, and development | Owned work, role-specific targets, checked work, coaching and review dates |
| One-off work becoming scalable | Sample spreadsheet-origin checklist, completed pilot, versioned process draft |
| Instrumented recruiting funnel | Explicit stage events, cohort membership, drop-off reasons, waiting work |
| Fast, effective onboarding | One editable checklist with requirements and first-opportunity support |
| Cross-functional collaboration | Concrete Marketing, Product, Ops, and Data tasks linked to a program |
| Industry fluency | Separate proceeding, attendance, service requirements, verified capabilities, credential records, and availability |
| Own a metric end-to-end | Baseline → target → plan → execution → observed result → reviewed decision |

## v2 minimum scope

All five tabs must be meaningful, but not equally elaborate. Markets and Recruiting carry the main diagnosis. Reporters and Team are compact working grids with detail panels. Programs supports one running test, one completed pilot proposed for expansion, and one stopped effort.

One shared evidence panel makes every important number or flag reconstructible. One main LAX storyline connects the tabs. Approximately 80–100 fictional people with complete histories are enough; record completeness is more important than volume.

Include eight complete reporting weeks of bounded historical events and a labeled future scenario. Five markets must have different supported constraints, not five copies of the same fixture.

## Explicit exclusions

No production backend, authentication, external API connector, live collaborative editing, real emails/SMS, true Google Sheets integration, full scheduling optimizer, legal eligibility engine, autonomous hiring/rejection, opaque quality score, speculative AI layer, new map, or company-wide forecasting platform.

A program-board view is secondary. The grid is required; a board using the same data can follow after the critical story passes. A workflow designer is out of scope; one configurable onboarding checklist and one saved process draft are sufficient.

## Industry boundary

Use separate fields for proceeding (for example deposition), attendance (remote/in person), and service requirement (for example realtime transcription). Credential labels such as RPR or CRR may be illustrative record values, but this packet makes no assertion that a particular credential is legally required or sufficient in a jurisdiction. Any required credential in the scenario is explicitly a **sample request requirement**.

No actual reporter, law-firm, or client data. Sample names, goals, policies, results, and operational thresholds must be disclosed as fictional. No claim that Steno currently uses the portrayed workaround, recruitment source, or internal workflow.

## Definition of done for the product, not this planning phase

A reviewer can start from a market gap, reproduce it from requests and person records, act on the relevant work, see what changed and what did not, and inspect a program decision supported by complete sample membership. None of those steps requires trusting a prewritten conclusion.
